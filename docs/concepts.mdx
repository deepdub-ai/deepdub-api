## voicePromptId — emotion-per-speaker model

A `voicePromptId` is not a single voice. It represents a specific speaker delivering in a specific emotional or stylistic mode. Most speakers in the catalog have several `voicePromptId`s: a neutral version, a happy version, a sad version, a "question" version with rising intonation, spontaneous/conversational, and others.

When you switch `voicePromptId`s between sentences in your application, you are switching the emotional delivery of the same speaker — not switching speakers.

**This is the primary lever for emotional control.** Customers coming from tag-based systems (where `<happy>`, `<sad>` are inline tags) should think of `voicePromptId` switching as the equivalent: instead of inline tags, you select the prompt that matches the mood for each sentence.

### Question intonation

Most speakers have a dedicated "question" `voicePromptId`. When your application generates a question sentence — particularly output from an LLM — switch to the question `voicePromptId` for that sentence. Without it, the model reads the question with statement intonation, which is one of the more jarring failure modes for voice agents.

### Roadmap

Inline tags that switch `voicePromptId` mid-sentence within a single request are in development. Today you achieve multi-emotion delivery by sending one request per emotion and concatenating the audio yourself. When inline switching ships, you will write a single sentence with multiple `voicePromptId`s inside it and receive one continuous audio response.

## Public model vs. private (dedicated) model

### Public model

Shared infrastructure, pay-per-call. The simplest way to get started. Available in US and EU regions. Billing is consumption-based: you pay for what you generate.

### Private (dedicated) model

A model instance reserved for your account. Named after your account (e.g., "VoiceSpin 2"). Versioning is independent per customer — your private model evolves separately from the public model. Billing is reservation-based (see [Concurrency and billing](#concurrency-and-billing)).

### When to choose

| Situation | Recommended |
|-----------|-------------|
| Bursty, low-volume, or experimental | Public |
| Steady high-volume voice agent or call-center | Private |
| Both patterns | Hybrid (private during peak hours, public as overflow) |

## Concurrency and billing

### Concurrency is reservation, not consumption

If you have a concurrency package, you pay for guaranteed capacity to start a generation immediately. The formula is:

```
1,000 credits × available minutes per day × concurrency units = daily cost
```

A concurrency=1 reservation, available 24 hours, costs `1,000 × 1,440 × 1 = 1,440,000 credits/day` — regardless of whether you make zero requests or thousands.

<Warning>
  If you see a large number on your dashboard (e.g., 1,440,000 credits), this is your **capacity reservation cost**, not audio you generated. Concurrency billing is constant.
</Warning>

### Effective throughput per concurrency unit

Generation runs 3–5× faster than real-time (a 9-second sentence generates in ~3 seconds). **1 concurrency unit supports roughly 4–6 simultaneous calls.** Size your concurrency with that ratio in mind — not 1:1.

### Time-to-first-audio

Expected time-to-first-audio on a private model: **100–300 ms**.

### Provisioning time

Spinning up new concurrency capacity takes **8–10 minutes** (AWS provisioning + model warm-up). Provision ahead of expected peaks — do not expect instant scale-up.

### Reading your usage dashboard

The dashboard updates once per day at 12:00 AM UTC. Same-day numbers appear after rollover.

| Row | What it means |
|-----|---------------|
| Concurrency usage | Your reservation cost. Constant per day at your fixed concurrency level. |
| TTS generation (API) | Public model consumption. Varies by traffic. |

### Public model billing

Public model usage is the opposite of concurrency packages: you pay only for what you generate, measured in generated audio duration.
