## Two ways to consume Deepdub TTS

One decision. Here's what each option is, how each is billed, and how to pick.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
<Card title="Public queue" icon="globe">
  Shared inference pool. Send TTS requests, get audio. We handle all the capacity. Pay only for audio generated.
</Card>
<Card title="Private (dedicated model)" icon="server">
  Reserved inference units. You pick N concurrency units and own the queue. Pay per reserved minute.
</Card>
</CardGroup>

---

## Mode A — Public queue

Shared inference pool. Send a request, get audio back. We manage capacity, scaling, and routing — you manage nothing.

- **No concurrency management.** Every request is just an HTTP or WebSocket call.
- **Pay per second of audio generated.** 60 seconds of output = 1,000 credits.
- **Shared with all other customers.** Peaks elsewhere can affect your latency.

---

## Mode B — Dedicated model (Enterprise)

A private inference pool reserved for you. You request N concurrency units and we hold capacity.

- **You pick the concurrency level.** Scale up before peak, down after.
- **Pay per minute of reserved capacity.** 1 concurrency for 60 min = 60,000 credits.
- **Isolated from public load.** Predictable latency, your own queue.

---

## What concurrency actually means

**Concurrency is a commitment, not a call cap.**

`Concurrency = N` means: we guarantee that at any moment, N requests can be in flight simultaneously without waiting. If a slot is free, the next request starts processing immediately. If N+1 requests arrive at exactly the same instant, request N+1 waits until one of the in-flight slots frees up.

<Info>
  Concurrency is **not** the same as "number of simultaneous calls." One concurrency slot can comfortably handle several live calls. See the sizing section below.
</Info>

## How a request travels

<Steps>

<Step title="Your bot sends a TTS request">
  Text, voice ID, locale, and target gender — sent over REST or WebSocket.
</Step>

<Step title="The request lands in the queue">
  If a slot is free, it's picked up instantly. If all N slots are busy, it waits.
</Step>

<Step title="An inference slot generates audio">
  Time-to-first-audio: ~200–300 ms.
</Step>

<Step title="Audio streams back to your bot">
  Typically 3–5× faster than the audio's playback length.
</Step>

<Step title="The slot frees up">
  The slot is free as soon as generation finishes — even while your bot is still playing the audio to the caller.
</Step>

</Steps>

## Why one slot serves several live calls

Two things are true about voice-bot traffic that mean concurrency goes much further than 1:1:

### Reason 1 — A call has silence

The bot only speaks part of the time. While the caller speaks, the model isn't generating anything for that call. In a typical conversation, the bot speaks roughly half the time.

Across N concurrent calls, the bot's speaking windows are staggered in time — they don't line up at the same millisecond. That stagger is what lets one slot serve multiple calls without any single caller waiting.

### Reason 2 — Generation beats playback

A 9-second sentence is generated in ~3 seconds. The slot is free during the remaining 6 seconds while your bot streams the audio to the caller.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Generation speed | 3–5× faster than playback |
| Time to first audio | 200–300 ms |
| Slot free time per request | ~67% of audio length |

## Sizing worked examples

### Scenario A — Concurrency = 1

| Step | Time |
|------|------|
| Generation time (9-second sentence) | ~3 s |
| Playback time | 9 s |
| Slot free after | 3 s |
| Slot idle (can serve another call) | 6 s |

During those 6 idle seconds, the same slot can accept a request from another call.

### Scenario B — Concurrency = 100

- 100 slots, each busy ~⅓ of the time per generation
- Add call-side silence (caller's turn): another ~½ reduction
- **Effective capacity: several hundred concurrent calls**

<Warning>
  Concurrency = 100 does not mean 100 calls maximum. It means at most 100 sentences being generated at the same instant. Size from peak concurrent **generations**, not peak concurrent calls.
</Warning>

**How to size:** Instrument your in-flight generation count and tune from real data. Start from your peak concurrent TTS requests — not your peak concurrent callers.

## Provisioning

Requesting concurrency is a REST call. Deepdub then spins up GPU instances on AWS to back those slots.

Cold-start time depends on AWS capacity at that moment — usually a few minutes, sometimes longer for large jumps.

| Time | Event |
|------|-------|
| t = 0 | You call the concurrency-set endpoint |
| +30s | Deepdub schedules GPU instances on AWS |
| +2–5m | Models warm up; new slots come online |
| Ready | All N slots serve traffic instantly |

<Warning>
  **Pre-warm capacity at least 5 minutes before your expected peak.** For large jumps (10× and up), pre-warm 15 minutes ahead. Do not wait for the load to arrive first.
</Warning>

## Billing — public queue

We meter the **length of the audio output**. That's the only signal — we don't see calls, sessions, or users.

| Usage | Credits |
|-------|---------|
| 60 seconds of audio | 1,000 credits |
| 30 seconds of audio | 500 credits |
| 6 seconds of audio | 100 credits |

**Example day (public model):**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Calls handled | 4,000 |
| Avg. bot speech per call | 45 seconds |
| Total audio generated | 180,000 s (3,000 min) |
| Credits consumed | 3,000,000 |
| What you manage | Nothing |

---

## Billing — dedicated model

When you request concurrency, the clock starts. We bill per **minute of reserved slot**, whether or not you use it.

| Usage | Credits |
|-------|---------|
| 1 concurrency · 1 hour | 60,000 credits |
| 1 concurrency · 1 day (24h) | 1,440,000 credits |

The formula: `1,000 credits × concurrency units × reserved minutes = cost`

<Info>
  With high utilization, you get significantly more audio output per credit than the public rate — because generation is 3–5× faster than playback.
</Info>

**Example day (dedicated model):**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Reserved concurrency | 10 slots |
| Hours reserved | 10 hours (business day) |
| Audio generated | Doesn't affect billing |
| Credits | 6,000,000 (10 × 10h × 60,000) |
| What you manage | Concurrency level + when to scale |

## Public vs. dedicated at a glance

| | Public queue | Dedicated model |
|--|-------------|-----------------|
| **Who shares it** | All Deepdub customers | Only you |
| **Latency under load** | Can be affected by peaks elsewhere | Predictable — you own the queue |
| **Billing model** | Per second of audio generated | Per minute of reserved slot |
| **Concurrency management** | None — we handle it | You set N; pre-warm before peaks |
| **Region availability** | US · EU (rolling out) | US and EU |
| **Best fit for** | Early-stage, bursty, low-volume, off-peak | Production voice bots, peak hours, latency-critical |

## The hybrid pattern

Use both. Dedicated for peak, public for the long tail.

A common production setup:

- **Business hours → dedicated model.** Predictable latency, sized for your peak.
- **Overnight / low volume → public queue.** No reserved capacity wasted.
- **Overflow → public queue.** If dedicated is saturated and a delay is acceptable, spill to public.

The split is enforced in your code by switching the endpoint and model name per request.

**Example week:**

| Time | Mode |
|------|------|
| Mon–Fri, 09:00–17:00 | Dedicated (N = 50) |
| Evenings + weekends | Public queue |

## What to build on your side

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="In-flight dashboard">
  Track requests sent, time-of-send, time-of-first-audio, and time-of-completion. Plot concurrent in-flight count over time. This is your primary sizing signal.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Pre-warm schedule">
  A cron or scheduler that bumps concurrency 5–15 minutes before known peaks, then scales down after.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Routing layer">
  A flag per request to choose dedicated vs. public. Default to dedicated; fall back to public when saturated or off-hours.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Pre-recorded greeting">
  Cache the first sentence as audio. Saves credits, locks in a perfect first impression, and hides any cold-start latency.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="generationId logging">
  Store the `generationId` returned per request. It's what Deepdub needs to debug any issue end-to-end. See [Errors](/errors) for guidance.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

## Key takeaways

1. **Concurrency is a commitment to N parallel in-flight generations** — not a cap on calls.
2. **One slot serves several live calls** because calls have silence and generation is 3–5× faster than playback.
3. **Public = pay per second of audio, no management.** Dedicated = pay per minute of reserved slot, you manage scale.
4. **Pre-warm 5–15 minutes before peaks.** GPU provisioning is not instant.
5. **Hybrid is common.** Dedicated during business hours, public overnight.
6. **Size from generations, not calls.** Instrument first, then tune.
